\section{Iterating the architecture: a theory-guided search}
\label{sec:search}

The three axes are not a taxonomy but a design space, and the results above
are predictions about which moves in it should pay. We tested that by running
a small architecture search in which every claim of
Sections~\ref{sec:depth}--\ref{sec:state} is a switch next to its naive
alternative, under a protocol fixed before the first candidate was scored.

\subsection{Architecture, benchmark, protocol}

\paragraph{Architecture.} A four-block residual model of width $64$ in which
each block's sequence mixer is one of \texttt{diag} (input-controlled diagonal
gating, the action of gated linear attention), \texttt{dense} (a static
dense transition with a per-token multiplicative gate, the shape of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hmm}), or \texttt{attn} (causal softmax attention). The
\emph{time} axis adds $r$ latent loops over thought slots that either
overwrite or append (Corollary~\ref{cor:crossover}) with optional codebook
projection every $k$ loops (Proposition~\ref{prop:kone}); the \emph{depth}
axis adds an early-exit auxiliary loss $\lambda$ on the shared head
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-eetrain}).

\paragraph{Benchmark.} One procedurally generated task per axis on a shared
vocabulary: \textsc{recall} (six key/value pairs, then a query; state
capacity), \textsc{track} (cyclic-HMM next-symbol prediction scored as the
fraction of headroom captured against the forward algorithm; state mixing),
and \textsc{compose} (eight generators of $S_5$ to their product; serial
time). Depth is the corrected-cost early-exit speedup $S$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cost} at an imposed head fraction $u = 0.26$, measured
on all three tasks. Fitness is $F = \text{state} + \text{time} + \text{depth}
\in [0,3]$ with depth credit gated by task quality, since an untrained model
has every layer near-uniform and would otherwise score full depth credit for
predicting nothing.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Parameters $\le 1.25\times$ and compute $\le 3.5\times$
a baseline reference, enforced before any compute is spent. Two seeds per
candidate, three for the baseline, held-out evaluation on fresh samples. The
noise band $\tau$ is the baseline's across-seed standard deviation of $F$,
fixed before any candidate runs; a candidate replaces the incumbent iff it
beats it by more than $\tau$. Every candidate is tagged with the axis it
mutates and the result that motivates it. The first three candidates are the
three theory-predicted moves, each applied to the naive baseline in
isolation, so each axis's contribution is measured before any are combined.

\subsection{Search v1: thirteen rounds, one accepted move, and why}

On the naive baseline ($F = 0.813 \pm 0.097$, so $\tau = 0.097$) the
early-exit loss was accepted at $+0.302$, moving $S$ from $1.12$ to $1.67$
while leaving every task score unchanged --- the effect of
Section~\ref{sec:exp-eetrain} reproduced on a different model and different
tasks. Dense mixing moved \textsc{track} in the predicted direction
($0.51 \to 0.57$) but landed at $+0.084$, under the band. Two appended loops
were \emph{worse} by $0.136$ at three times the compute.

Ten further rounds --- stacking, more $\lambda$, two attention layers, deeper
exits only, all-dense, two learning rates, a matched append-versus-overwrite
pair, projection every step --- produced nothing above the band. The
diagnosis, reached by diagnostics rather than assumed, was that two axes were
pinned by the benchmark: \textsc{recall} sat at $0.33$--$0.36$ for
\emph{every} layout including four attention layers trained on it alone with
four times the budget, and \textsc{compose} reached $0.27$ at best. A
localiser sweeping the number of pairs found the pipeline sound (a one-pair
copy trains to $1.000$) and the failure beginning at two pairs, the first
point a key match is required: two-hop retrieval does not form in this model
at this budget. Architecture cannot register on tasks no architecture can
learn. Two results from v1 stand on their own: the matched loop test went
\emph{against} Corollary~\ref{cor:crossover} (overwrite beat append by
$0.104$ at $r=1$, both worse than no loop), and projecting every step on a
noise-free loop changed nothing ($0.996$ against $0.998$), as
Proposition~\ref{prop:kone} says it should not.

\subsection{Search v2: the same rule on a learnable encoding}

v2 changed the \textsc{recall} encoding to one token per pair --- a one-hop
content match with identical semantics --- and nothing else, after a gate
showed all-attention now climbs ($0.27 \to 0.42 \to 0.66$ and rising) where
diagonal state does not ($0.375$). The v2 baseline scored $0.934 \pm 0.015$,
so $\tau_2 = 0.015$. Table~\ref{tab:search} gives the pre-committed rounds.

Three things follow. The depth move is the most robust prediction in this
paper: $+0.302$ in both searches, on both encodings, with the task scores
untouched. Dense mixing pays exactly where Theorem~\ref{thm:noembed} and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-bite} say it should --- \textsc{track} rose from $0.55$
to $0.63$ alone and to $0.68$ combined --- while \textsc{recall} did not move
with or without an attention layer in the three-task mix, so the recall
layer may be dead weight; round~20 tests that. And latent loops have hurt at
every budget and encoding tried, with the append-versus-overwrite comparison
going against Corollary~\ref{cor:crossover} twice. The corollary is a
capacity statement about long horizons; at $r = 1$ on a model that cannot yet
use one loop, capacity is not what binds, and we record the loss rather than
explain it away. The round-19 incumbent carries a wide spread across its two
seeds; the final claim is made on seeds the search never saw.
