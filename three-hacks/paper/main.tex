\begin{abstract}
Three recurring complaints about autoregressive language models --- that
speculative decoding needs an auxiliary draft network, that reasoning is
externalised into emitted tokens, and that decoder-only transformers are
elaborate recurrent networks --- are usually treated separately. We show they
are three instances of one question: along which axis is a model permitted to
spend \emph{serial} computation, and how does it carry state while doing so.
We formalise each along its own axis (depth, time, state) and prove what the
informal complaint does and does not license.
For depth, we show speculative sampling is exact for an arbitrary draft
distribution, so correctness imposes no constraint on the draft; we give a cost
model interpolating between memory- and compute-bound decoding under which
self-drafting by early exit dominates any external draft of equal relative cost,
and lower-bound the acceptance rate by the residual tail energy.
For time, we show the strong form of the latent-reasoning complaint is false
under standard complexity assumptions --- serial compute is load-bearing --- but
that a counting argument separates the \emph{media}: a chain-of-thought step
transports at most $\log_2|\Vocab|$ bits, a latent step transports the state
dimension. Discretisation, not serial computation, is the object of the
complaint.
For state, we show the transformer's recurrent form is real but nearly vacuous,
that a constrained gated linear recurrence coincides exactly with the HMM
forward algorithm, and --- our main negative result --- that
\emph{diagonally}-gated linear attention, the form used by most modern
sub-quadratic architectures, provably cannot realise a mixing HMM transition
within a single layer. We close with a capacity lower bound that fixes what
unbounded state is buying.
All claims are stated to be falsifiable, and we give a protocol at $\le$1B
parameters with pre-registered kill criteria.
\end{abstract}
